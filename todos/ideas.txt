Regulation / Rule
	What It Covers

	1% US Federal Remittance Excise Tax (IRC § 4475, effective Jan 1, 2026)
	Outbound transfers sent from the US to foreign countries (e.g., US students paying UK or Chinese tuition)
	Imposes a 1% federal excise tax on outbound remittances over $15 only when funded with physical cash, money orders, or cashier's checks. Transfers funded via a US bank account (ACH/wire) or a US-issued debit/credit card are 100% exempt under IRC § 4475(d)(1).
	Instruct US students paying UK or Chinese universities to fund transfers exclusively via a US checking/savings account or US debit/credit card (never cashier's checks or cash) to pay $0 tax.
	HIGH (US Outbound Alert)
	IRS Form 3520 (Foreign Gifts > $100,000) & IRC § 2503(e) Tuition Exemption


	Large gifts/support received from foreign persons
	A "US Person" for tax purposes receiving > $100,000 from a foreign individual in a calendar year must file informational Form 3520.


• 5-Year F-1 Exemption: International students on F-1/J-1 visas are Non-Resident Aliens (exempt individuals) during their first 5 calendar years in the US and do not file Form 3520.


• Direct Tuition Exemption (IRC § 2503(e)): Even for US citizens/residents or Year 6+ F-1 students, tuition paid directly to the university by a foreign parent is exempt from Form 3520.
	If a student is a US citizen, green card holder, or in Year 6+ on an F-1 visa and receives >$100,000/year in total parental support, advise parents to pay tuition directly to the university (via Flywire, Convera, or direct wire) rather than wiring >$100,000 into the student's personal US bank account[cite: 1, 5].
	HIGH (Smart Tax Routing)
	FATCA & FinCEN FBAR (FinCEN Form 114)


	Reporting of foreign bank accounts by US persons
	US tax residents holding > $10,000 in aggregate foreign bank accounts at any point during the year must file an FBAR (and potentially Form 8938 for FATCA). F-1/J-1 students in their first 5 calendar years are exempt.
	Warn US students studying in the UK or China who open a local UK or Chinese bank account to pay $50,000 tuition (Route 1B) that holding >$10,000 in a foreign account triggers a free annual FBAR filing[cite: 1, 5].
	HIGH (US-to-UK/China Alert)
	Bank Secrecy Act (BSA), FinCEN & OFAC Screening


	AML/KYC monitoring and US sanctions screening
	Banks monitor large transfers, file Currency Transaction Reports (for physical cash > $10,000) and Suspicious Activity Reports (SARs), and screen all USD payments against OFAC sanctions lists.
	Use regulated services and include clear purpose ("Tuition" + Student ID) alongside student documents[cite: 1, 5].
	LOW (Automated)
	Source of Funds / University Compliance


	University due diligence on incoming international wires
	Universities perform AML checks on large incoming wires and enforce strict "return-to-source" refund rules.
	Paying via Flywire, Convera, or official university portals reduces manual cashier holds and keeps a clean paper trail[cite: 1, 5].

	Section 3: Upcoming & Newly Enacted Laws (Late 2026–2028 Pipeline) Affecting Your App
1. China PBoC Announcement [2026] No. 9 on Unlicensed Overseas Financial Marketing (Effective September 30, 2026)
* The Rule: Effective September 30, 2026, Announcement [2026] No. 9 (公告〔2026〕第9号) explicitly classifies "an overseas institution providing financial-product services to domestic residents without a licence" as an illegal financial activity that no institution or individual may market or facilitate online inside Mainland China. Furthermore, providers such as Payoneer, Airwallex, PayPal, PingPong, and LianLian hold domestic entities on the PBoC payment-institution register, whereas offshore-only fintech accounts (like Wise or WorldFirst) are not listed as domestic PBoC payment institutions.
* Impact on Your App: If your app is marketed to users located inside Mainland China, do not actively market or provide direct onboarding referral links for unlicensed offshore bank/fintech accounts to Chinese domestic residents. For users inside Mainland China, display PBoC-compliant/university-authorized education channels (Flywire, Convera, EasyTransfer, Alipay, UnionPay, and Chinese commercial bank SWIFT wires)[cite: 1, 3], and reserve Wise/Revolut local-account routing recommendations for corridors between the UK and US (or for students who have already arrived overseas and hold valid foreign visas/residency).
2. Global SWIFT ISO 20022 Structured Address Mandate (Effective November 2026)
* The Rule: In November 2026, SWIFT is retiring unstructured free-text address fields (MT format) for cross-border payments and enforcing strict ISO 20022 structured/hybrid postal addresses (StreetName, BuildingNumber, PostCode, TownName, Country) alongside standardized Purpose Codes.
* Impact on Your App: If your app displays or generates downloadable SWIFT wire instruction sheets (Route 1A) for universities, any beneficiary address formatted as an unstructured block of text risks automatic wire rejection or intermediary repair deductions ($25–$50) after November 2026. Store all university bank details in strict ISO 20022 structured fields.
3. UK FCA CASS 15 Safeguarding Rules (May 2026) & Open Banking Variable Recurring Payments (2026–2027)
* The Rules:
   * CASS 15 Safeguarding Regime (May 2026 FCA Approach Document): The FCA introduced strengthened safeguarding, statutory trust, resolution pack (CASS 10A), and monthly regulatory return requirements (SUP) for UK payment and e-money institutions.
   * UK Open Finance & Variable Recurring Payments (VRPs) (2026–2027 Roadmap): Under the UK's Data (Use and Access) framework, the FCA and HM Treasury are expanding Open Banking VRPs into lower-cost commercial payment rails ahead of a full Open Finance framework by late 2027.
   * FCA BNPL Regulation (Mid-2026 onward): Third-party deferred tuition instalment credit in the UK now requires FCA consumer credit authorization.
* Impact on Your App: UK Open Banking VRPs will allow your app to help students set up automated, $0-fee GBP tuition instalment payments directly from UK bank accounts. Avoid offering or promoting unregulated "split-tuition credit/BNPL" products in the UK without an FCA-authorized credit partner.
4. US CFPB Section 1033 Open Banking Rulemaking (2026–2027 Revision)
* The Rule: Following its August 2026 submission to OIRA, the CFPB is finalizing revised Dodd-Frank Section 1033 (Personal Financial Data Rights) rules governing how fintech apps access US consumer bank accounts via APIs (such as Plaid or Stripe) to verify balances and trigger 0% US ACH eChecks. The rule enforces strict data minimization, annual re-authorization, and bans using payment data for targeted cross-selling.
* Impact on Your App: If you integrate Plaid or Open Banking to verify student funds or initiate ACH tuition payments, restrict data collection strictly to the payment workflow and build an automatic 12-month consent renewal prompt.
5. China PIPL & CAC Cross-Border Data Transfer Rules (Ongoing Enforcement)
* The Rule: Under China's Personal Information Protection Law (PIPL) and Cyberspace Administration of China (CAC) cross-border data rules, financial account numbers, National ID cards, passports, and tuition invoices are classified as Sensitive Personal Information.
* Impact on Your App: Never upload or store Chinese students' KYC documents (National IDs, Passports, Bank Statements, I-20s) on overseas cloud servers. Keep your document checklist generator 100% client-side (local to the user's phone/browser) and let students upload their documents directly inside the university portal, Flywire, Convera, or their bank's app[cite: 1, 3].
